# Visual finish gate

Use this gate after section screenshots exist and before calling the page finished. Mechanical validity is necessary but does not prove art direction.

## Full-page silhouette

Capture one full-page desktop image and one full-page phone image. Blur or zoom them out until copy is unreadable, then judge only mass, brightness, rhythm, and focal changes.

Fail when:

- every section is the same height, alignment, or text/media split;
- a saturated accent fills multiple consecutive screens without a quieter material field;
- the page alternates only between giant text and rectangular video;
- the Hero could advertise an unrelated speaker, AI startup, perfume, car, or fashion label without changing the subject;
- every visual idea is equally loud, leaving no dominant peak or recovery beat.

## Chinese display silhouette

Review the rendered line shapes, not only font names.

- Keep semantic phrases together. Do not strand one Han character or punctuation on its own line unless it is an intentional poster device approved in the plan.
- A Chinese display block longer than eight characters normally needs a container wide enough for 4–8 Han characters per line on desktop and 3–6 on phone.
- Reject stair-step wrapping created by an undersized column, excessive font size, or generic `text-wrap: balance`.
- Let a calligraphic line breathe. Avoid extreme negative tracking, clipped ascenders, edge collisions, and several equally large calligraphic blocks on one screen.
- Capture the full block at desktop and phone; a type specimen outside the final layout is insufficient.
- Run a final-font metric preflight first: for the worst desktop and phone line in every signature/support-display module, record final glyph width and final container content width. Reject ratios above 1. Treat this as an overflow detector only; screenshots still decide collision, rhythm, and visual quality.

## Color and material budget

Estimate visible page area from the full-page captures.

- For a cinematic dark page, ink, charcoal, bone, concrete, or media-derived neutrals should normally occupy 65–85% of the long-page silhouette.
- A high-chroma accent normally occupies 5–20%. It may briefly dominate one intentional beat, but not multiple consecutive sections.
- Treat colored light in video differently from a flat CSS fill. Prefer light, reflection, haze, grain, metal, paper, or fabric over a featureless saturated rectangle.
- Reject pure accent fields that make the page resemble a presentation template or default campaign microsite.

## Media specificity

For each generated asset write one sentence explaining why it belongs to this subject. Reject generic glowing rings, isolated product pedestals, neon tunnels, abstract particles, and empty sci-fi rooms when they function only as fashionable filler.

The Hero must communicate at least two subject-specific signals through material, setting, gesture, or camera behavior. If not, regenerate it or assign a more specific existing asset to Hero.

## Final pass

Pass only when:

1. both full-page captures have a clear dominant peak, two or more contrast beats, and a calm ending;
2. no unintended single-character display line appears at desktop or phone;
3. the high-chroma area stays inside the declared budget;
4. the Hero passes the unrelated-brand substitution test;
5. all section screenshots still pass focal safety, contrast, and typography checks.
